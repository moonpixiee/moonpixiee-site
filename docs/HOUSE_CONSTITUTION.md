# 0. Purpose

House of Luna is not a portfolio.

It is not a landing page.

It is not a storefront.

It is not a personal brand website.

House of Luna is a digital house.

Every page is a room.

Every room has its own atmosphere, purpose, and personality while remaining unmistakably part of the same architecture.

Visitors should feel like they are wandering through a place—not navigating a website.

Whenever implementation and this document disagree, this document wins.

---

# 1. Brand Hierarchy

House of Luna, Luna, and Moonpixiee are not competing brands.

They are three layers of the same identity.

House of Luna
→ the place, the umbrella brand, the website, the ecosystem

Luna
→ the founder and resident of the House

Moonpixiee
→ Luna's creative signature, author identity, and publishing voice

Global navigation and primary site branding use House of Luna.

Moonpixiee appears where authorship matters:

Book authorship

Quote attribution

Letters from Moonpixiee

Substack

The Library imprint

Selective signature moments

Moonpixiee is a signature.

A signature is not a logo.

Moonpixiee must never function as the repeated site-wide wordmark.

---

# 2. Core Philosophy

House of Luna is built around exploration.

Nothing should feel algorithmic.

Nothing should feel corporate.

Nothing should feel optimized for engagement.

Curiosity should guide navigation.

The House rewards wandering.

---

# 3. The House Remembers

This is a foundational principle.

Not a feature.

A house does not reset when you leave it.

The bookmark stays in the book.

The plant is still alive.

Nothing has moved.

A website that remembers nothing is behaving like a page, not a place.

The House remembers its visitors.

What the House remembers

Where you left off

What you saved

What you wrote

What you asked

What you prefer

What the House never does

Remember in order to sell

Remember in order to optimize

Remember in order to increase engagement

Remember anything the visitor would be surprised to learn it kept

The House remembers only what benefits the visitor.

Memory is hospitality.

Memory is never surveillance.

If a memory serves the House more than the visitor, the House does not keep it.

Default Theme:
Light

Secondary Theme:
Dark

Primary palette:

Smoke

System Red is a separate color from House Red.

**Amendment (v4.1 — Luna, 28 July 2026 — companion to §26 The Aesthetic Laws):**

# 6. The Red Thread

House Red is the visual thread connecting every room.

It appears differently depending on where the visitor is.

Library
→ annotation ink

Edit
→ lipstick / fashion

Machine Room
→ electrical systems

Luna's Room
→ personal marks

Moss & Moon
→ berries / botanical accents

The same color should communicate different emotions through context.

There is one House Red.

Not five.

The thread only holds if the color does not change.

House Red is a mark.

House Red is used for rules, underlines, annotations, inscriptions, and single-word emphasis.

House Red is never used for body copy.

House Red is never used for paragraphs.

House Red is never the only thing carrying a meaning.

# 7. Typography

Typography is hierarchical.

Editorial Serif
Primary reading voice.

Sans Serif
Navigation and interface.

Monospace
Machine Room only.

Gothic
Reserved for ceremonial moments.

Never use Gothic for body copy.

Never use Gothic for navigation.

Never use Gothic for paragraphs.

Gothic is used as inscription typography.

Examples:

L O V E

R E A D

B E C O M E

B U I L D

G R O W

Spacing between letters should be generous.

One inscription per room.

An inscription used twice is decoration.

# 17. Memory and Privacy

The House holds people's private writing.

That is a responsibility before it is a feature.

Rules

Visitors may read the House without a key.

Anonymous wandering is always permitted.

A key is requested only when the visitor tries to keep something.

Every visitor can see everything the House remembers about them.

Every visitor can export it.

Every visitor can delete it, completely and permanently.

Private writing is private.

Luna does not read visitors' journals.

Nothing written in a book is used for marketing, training, or analytics.

AI Luna and memory

AI Luna may only use what the visitor has explicitly allowed her to use.

Memory shared with AI Luna is opt-in.

Opt-in means the visitor chose it, not that the visitor failed to prevent it.

The visitor may revoke it at any time.

AI Luna never references a memory the visitor has not been told she holds.

Obligations

The House maintains a plain-language privacy policy.

The House complies with applicable data protection law.

Private writing is encrypted at rest.

If the House ever closes, visitors get their writing back before it does.

The test

If a visitor discovered everything the House remembers about them, they should feel cared for.

Not surprised.

Not exposed.

If a memory would fail that test, the House does not keep it.

# 26. The Aesthetic Laws

*Ratified — Luna, 28 July 2026. Folded from PHASE-R-AESTHETIC-LAWS.md (with LAW 1 amendment and LAW 2 clarification). Colors may evolve; these laws are immutable. Token values are governed by PHASE-R-TOKEN-PROPOSAL.md; retirements are preserved in `docs/design-history/`.*

## The Experience Test (governing acceptance criterion)

Every visual review answers to one sentence. A first-time visitor should think:

> *"…I've never been here before… but it feels like this place has been waiting for me."*

Not "that's pretty." Not "nice website." If a design decision moves the House toward this sentence, it is right; if it moves away, it is wrong, whatever else recommends it.

## LAW 1 — The House is illuminated, never brightly lit.

No harsh white UI. No sterile interfaces. Light always has a source: candles, lamps, windows, moonlight, daylight, or glowing machinery. Even the House's lightest spaces feel warm, dimensional, and inhabited.

*(Amended at ratification: the House's established visual direction is light-first. Evening remains an available atmosphere, not a global mandate.)*

## LAW 2 — Whenever darkness appears, it is a material, not an absence.

Black is not empty. It has texture: stone, velvet, old wood, paper, smoke. Every dark surface should feel touchable.

## LAW 3 — Red is sacred.

If everything is red, nothing is. House Red means invitation, focus, importance, love, transformation — never decoration.

**Corollary:** *House Red does not narrate. It invites.* House Red is a material before it is a text color. On dark grounds it speaks as Voice; on Paper it works as link and as ceremony, and it declines body copy — not as limitation, but as identity.

## LAW 4 — Gold whispers.

Never shiny, never luxury-for-luxury's-sake. Gold appears as worn brass, old frames, page edges, tiny details. If visitors consciously notice the gold, there is probably too much.

## LAW 5 — Rooms own color. The House owns light.

The hallway does not turn green because Moss & Moon exists. The Hall stays House. Each room's personality begins past its threshold. This is what keeps the House unified.

## LAW 6 — Nothing snaps into existence.

Not slow. Not floaty. Things settle, appear, breathe.

**Reduced-motion corollary:** the settled state is complete in itself; breathing is atmosphere, never information.

## LAW 7 — The House never shouts.

No ALL-CAPS navigation, no loud marketing typography. The largest heading feels like someone speaking across a quiet room.

**Italic corollary:** the soft italic voice is reserved for the Experience Test, brief emotional whispers, and occasional quotations. Instructional and explanatory sentences are set in the regular serif.

## LAW 8 — The House speaks with one voice. Its residents have accents.

**Corollary:** the House maintains one shared typographic cast. Rooms may alter rhythm, measure, spacing, hierarchy density, and sentence cadence, but they do not replace the cast at the threshold.

**Legibility corollary:** whispering may reduce emphasis, never legibility.

**The cast:** Narrator — Source Serif 4 · Architect — Cormorant Garamond · Librarian — Jost · Machine — IBM Plex Mono.

**Role boundary:** the Architect names. The Librarian counts and annotates. The Narrator receives and guides. The Machine reports.
