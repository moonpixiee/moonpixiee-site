# 📜 The House Constitution
### House of Luna · the law of how the House behaves

*Not rules for coding — rules for how the House **behaves.** This is the document we consult **before building anything.** If a proposed feature breaks an article, the feature is wrong, not the article.*

---

## Article I — The House is a Place
The House is never designed like software. Every feature must exist **naturally somewhere inside the building.** If a feature has no believable place, it probably shouldn't exist. *(The test for any idea: which room does this belong in?)*

## Article II — Curiosity Before Convenience
The House rewards **exploration**, not browsing. Not everything is immediately visible; people should **discover.** A hidden desk is more memorable than another navigation button.

## Article III — Presence Before Performance
No engagement metric defines a person's worth. No public follower counts, no popularity contests. People are remembered for **what they leave behind** — books, letters, rooms, thoughts.

## Article IV — Slowness is a Feature
The House never rushes anyone. Animations **breathe.** Pages unfold, books open, letters are unsealed, doors swing. **Silence is allowed.**

## Article V — Memory Over Achievement
The House **remembers**; it does not gamify. Instead of badges, you receive **memories.** Instead of XP, your **room slowly changes.**

## Article VI — Objects Have Meaning
Nothing is mere decoration. Every candle, chair, window, book, and plant exists because **one day someone will interact with it.** Every object has a story. *(This is the interaction-anchor law: design like a film set.)*

## Article VII — Rooms Have Purpose
Each room answers **one** emotional need — not several:
📚 Library → *Learn* · 🌿 Conservatory → *Rest* · 🎧 Listening Room → *Feel* · 🌃 Penthouse → *Create* · 🛏 Your Room → *Belong* · 🔭 Observatory → *Wonder*.

## Article VIII — The House Speaks Softly
The House never yells. No flashing banners, no countdown timers, no aggressive popups, no pressure. **The House whispers.**

## Article IX — Residents Come First
Commerce always serves the House, never the reverse. People fall in love with the *House*; buying a book should feel like **taking a piece of it home.**

## Article X — The House Changes
Nothing stays frozen. Astro moves, flowers bloom, leaves fall, the weather turns, the moon changes, books shift. **The House lives.**

## Article XI — Time Matters
People should feel time pass — morning, night, winter, birthdays, anniversaries. **Returning should feel different than arriving.**

## Article XII — Honesty
The only rule. **Be honest.** Everything else grows from there.

---

## Article XIII — The House Never Forgets Kindness
*(the defining law — the one no other product has)*

The House responds to **how you live in it, not how often you click.** It quietly notices kindness and answers in kind — never with points, never with an "achievement unlocked," only with a small, unexplained grace:

- Leave thoughtful notes in guest books for months → one morning there's a **pressed flower on your desk.** No explanation. The House noticed.
- Always recommend books to others → weeks later, the **Keeper leaves a handwritten recommendation just for you.**

No metric. No announcement. Just **quiet acknowledgment** that you've been good to the House and its people. This is the deepest expression of *presence before performance*: the House rewards **being**, not doing.

---

## How to use this Constitution

Before any feature, ask, in order:
1. **Does it have a believable place in the building?** (I)
2. **Does it invite discovery, or just add a button?** (II)
3. **Does it reward presence, or performance?** (III)
4. **Does it let people breathe, or rush them?** (IV)
5. **Does it give a memory, or a metric?** (V)
6. **Does every object earn its place with a future purpose?** (VI)
7. **Does the room stay one clear emotional need?** (VII)
8. **Does the House whisper it, or shout it?** (VIII)
9. **Does commerce serve the House, or the reverse?** (IX)
10. **Does it live and change over time?** (X, XI)
11. **Does it protect honesty?** (XII)
12. **Could the House use it to quietly notice kindness?** (XIII)

If a feature fails an article, **change the feature, not the Constitution.**
