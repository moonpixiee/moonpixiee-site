# 📖 The Resident's Handbook
### The social constitution of House of Luna

*Handed to you, quietly, when you arrive. This is where a community flourishes or falls apart — so we decide it on purpose, before there is a crowd.*

---

## Welcome in

You've found a place that isn't work and isn't home. A third place. You don't have to perform here. You don't have to post. You can read slowly, save what moves you, decorate a room, wander the halls, and leave small traces that you were here. **The House rewards presence, not performance.**

There is only **one rule**, and everything else is just how we keep it:

> ### The one rule: be honest — especially when it's uncomfortable.
> The House is built for people who tell the truth gently. Not cruelty dressed as honesty; not performance dressed as vulnerability. Just realness. It's the whole reason this place can feel safe.

---

## Who you are here

Not a demographic — an *identity*. The House is for people **curious enough to wander**: who read slowly, save quotes, collect beautiful things, romanticise ordinary life, create more than they consume, are quietly rebuilding themselves, and are honest even when it's uncomfortable. You don't come to escape life. You come to remember yourself before returning to it.

If that's you: you're already home. If it isn't yet: the House is patient.

---

## Your Room — what's yours, and what's private

Your Room is **yours**. It is the one place in the House that answers only to you.

- **Who can enter?** *You decide.* The default is a gentle open door — friends can knock and come in — but you set the House's oldest courtesy: **you can lock your room.** Locked means no one enters without you. You can also set it to **"Not tonight"** (a light-off state) — present, but not receiving.
- **What's public vs. private?** *You choose, per thing.* By default your **door plaque** (room name, current reading/listening, mood, Away/Home) is visible in the Hallways. Inside, **what shows is what you share**: your books, your music, your kept artifacts, your seasonal decor are shared unless you hide them; **your journal is private by default** — individual pages become visible only if you mark them *shared*.
- **What can a visitor do?** Look around. Read what you've shared. Play your current playlist. Sign your **Guest Book**. Leave you a **Letter**. That's all — a visitor can *touch nothing, take nothing, change nothing.* A room is only ever edited by the person who lives in it.
- **The House remembers your room for you** — it ages, keeps what you keep, holds your seasons. (See *The House Remembers*.)

---

## Visiting others — how to be a good guest

You wander the **Hallways**. You see doors, plaques, a light on here, music behind one there, sometimes **Astro** curled outside someone's door. You get **curious**. So:

- **You knock.** If they're open, the door opens and you're *inside their room* — not on a profile. Look around slowly.
- **You can leave two things, and only two:** a mark in their **Guest Book**, and a **Letter**.
- **You never see followers, counts, or "who else viewed this."** Curiosity, not surveillance.
- **You take nothing.** You can *keep the feeling* — save a book they love to your own Want-to-Read — but their things stay theirs.

---

## Letters — how people talk here

There are no DMs. There are **Letters.**

- To reach someone, you **write a Letter and slide it under their door.**
- They don't get a notification. They come home and find an **envelope** waiting — a small, good feeling, not a red dot.
- Letters are **private by default** (between you and them). A recipient may choose to **pin a Letter to their door** to make it public, if it's the kind of thing meant to be seen.
- Letters are **gentle by design** — no read-receipts-as-pressure, no typing indicators, no "seen 3h ago." A letter is patient. It waits to be answered, or not.

---

## The Guest Book — little traces

Every room has a **Guest Book**, like a museum or a bed-and-breakfast.

- A visitor writes something small: *"Stopped by tonight. Loved your room. ☾"* Short, kind, a trace.
- **No threads. No replies-to-replies. No likes.** It is not a comment section; it's a record that *someone was here, and it was nice.*
- **The room owner curates.** You can quietly remove anything from your own Guest Book. Your room, your record.

---

## Reading together — the warm, social heart

This is where the House becomes a community instead of a collection of quiet rooms.

- **Shared Reading Lists.** You can **share** a list, and a friend can **borrow** it — copy it to their own shelf as *Want to Read*. Recommending a book is an act of friendship here, not a promoted post.
- **Book Clubs.** A book club is a **shared shelf + a room to gather** (often the Café). People reading the same book at the same pace; a Guest-Book-style thread *scoped to that book*; maybe a **live reading night** where the Café fills with chairs and people are simply *present*, reading together.
- **The Café.** Sometimes someone is already sitting there, reading — a little book icon over their table shows what. You can **quietly join.** No pressure, no notification, no obligation to talk. The feeling that others are *around* — IMVU, not Discord.
- **Collaboration.** Two residents can choose to **open a door between their rooms** for a while — co-decorate a shared shelf, build a reading list together, keep a joint guest book. Always opt-in, always reversible.

---

## Becoming a Resident

You arrive a **guest**. You become a **resident** by *living here* — not by paying, not by a badge.

Somewhere along the way — after you've furnished your room, written a journal page, left your first note on someone's door — **the Keeper leaves a key on your desk**:

> *"You've been here long enough. Welcome home."*

That's not a level-up. It's **belonging**. A resident's room becomes a permanent part of the House; the House speaks to you a little more like family. Residency isn't a status you display — it's a fact you feel.

---

## When the one rule is broken

Honesty is the whole foundation, so cruelty, deception, harassment, or performing-for-harm is the one thing the House won't hold.

- **The Keeper handles it — quietly, never publicly.** No pile-ons, no public shaming (that would break the House worse than the offense).
- **A gradient, not a banhammer.** First, a **note from the Keeper** — a private word. Then, if it continues, the House gets **quiet around that person** — their letters don't slide as easily, their room dims in the halls. Finally, for real harm, a room is **closed** and the key taken back: *"This House wasn't for you after all."*
- **You can always lock your door, and always report a letter or a guest** to the Keeper. Safety is never a performance either — no public accusations, just a quiet word to the one who keeps the House.

---

## What the House will never do (so residents can trust it)

- ❌ Never show followers, following, or any count as status.
- ❌ Never an infinite feed, never an algorithm deciding what you see.
- ❌ Never likes, never engagement metrics, never notifications designed to pull you back.
- ❌ Never sell your attention, never let a room be "sponsored," never rank people.
- ✅ Only: rooms, guest books, letters, shared reading, book clubs, and "I visited."

---

## The closing line

House of Luna isn't trying to replace Instagram. It's trying to bring back the internet we miss — reimagined as a beautiful mansion where reading, creativity, and honest connection live together. It isn't finished. It's **inhabited.**

*Stay as long as you like. The fire keeps its own hours.* 🖤🌙
