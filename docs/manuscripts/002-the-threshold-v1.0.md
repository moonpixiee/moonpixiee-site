# 📖 HOUSE OF LUNA — ROOM MANUSCRIPT
## №002 — The Threshold · Version 1.0
### *Set 001 — the room you enter before the Library*

> *"Take your coat off. You're safe here."*

---

## I. The Soul of the Room

We called it the Library Entrance. It isn't. It is **The Threshold** — the place where the outside world *ends.*

When someone enters House of Luna, they are not hit with buttons, menus, and content. They are given **permission to arrive.** That is a feeling almost no place online still offers. Everything else screams *click this, buy this, watch this, hurry.* The House whispers: *"Take your coat off. You're safe here."*

The Threshold's job is not to inform or to route. Its job is a single feeling: **you have arrived somewhere safe, and nothing here needs anything from you yet.**

## II. The Story

The House has stood a thousand years, and the door has always been unlocked — not for everyone, but for anyone who found their way to it. Countless people have crossed this threshold, shed the day at the door, and stepped into the quiet. The Threshold has welcomed every one of them the same way: without a word, without hurry, with the fire already lit somewhere deeper in.

## III. The Visitor's Journey — *the first few seconds*

The doors open. **Silence.** Fire, somewhere in the distance. Moonlight. Dust drifting through the air. A raven perched impossibly high above. Candles flickering. Books stretching farther than the eye can see. Someone turns a page, somewhere — but no one is visible.

Then… **nothing.** No tutorial. No popup. No celebration. *(Law 007 — The House Never Rushes You.)* The House trusts that you'll look around.

**After a few seconds,** the Keeper is simply *there* — not a dialogue box, not an interruption. She waits. If you're ready, she offers the House's first and only greeting:

> *"Welcome home."*

Not "Welcome to House of Luna." Not "Let's get started." Just — *welcome home.* And only then, when you've already arrived, does she ask, gently:

> *"Would you like to study by the fire tonight?"* — or — *"Would you rather wander?"*

Now you are making a **choice inside a place**, not navigating an interface.

## IV. The Emotional Palette

Never: rushed · salesy · loud · gamified · impressive · busy.
Instead: **welcomed · unhurried · safe · hushed · held · trusted.**

## V. Architecture

A great threshold at night. Tall doors, open — **warm light spilling from deeper inside.** Beyond them, the Library begins: a vestibule of dark stone and wood that gives way to shelves climbing into a ceiling lost to shadow. High, cold windows let the moon in; far ahead, a warm fire glows, pulling the eye — and the feet — forward. The whole space **leans inward**, toward the warmth, toward the House.

## VI. Set Dressing

**The Doors.** Old, heavy, hand-worn — standing **ajar.** You cannot see the room beyond, only that it is warm in there and someone left it open *for you.* (An open door shows too much; a closed door refuses; an ajar door is the whole feeling of the House.)

**The Coat Hook.** Just inside — a single iron hook, and on it, a coat someone already hung. *Take your coat off. You're safe here.* Whose coat, no one knows.

**The Spill of Light.** Warm firelight and candleglow leaking from deeper in, laid in a long soft rectangle across the stone floor — the path forward, drawn in light.

**Dust in the Moonbeam.** Slow motes drifting where cold moonlight crosses warm light. Proof the air is still and the room is old.

**The Distant Fire.** Never fully seen from here — only its glow, and the promise of it.

**The First Stair / The Rug.** Underfoot, a worn rug; ahead, the first step deeper in.

## VII. The Cast

**The Keeper — and one flag for the gate.** Your vision has the Keeper *walk into view* and say *"Welcome home."* The Keeper Bible's oldest law is that she is **intentionally unknowable** — no visible avatar, expressed only through evidence and the House's voice. To honor **both**, my Creative-Director proposal: at the Threshold — and *only* here, as the sacred arrival — the Keeper appears as a **half-seen presence at the edge of the candlelight.** You feel her more than you see her: the hem of a dark dress, a shape between the shelves, a warmth. Never a face. She says *"Welcome home,"* and by the time you look directly, she has stepped back into the House. This keeps her folklore intact while giving you the welcome. *(Flag: confirm this reconciliation, or tell me the Threshold is the one place she's fully seen — your call, and it's canon either way.)*

**Astro.** Optional — perhaps glimpsed at the top of the stairs, deciding whether you're allowed. Never staged.

**Keeper Trace.** The coat on the hook. A candle just lit. The door held open. She was here a moment before you.

## VIII. Sound

Near silence. The distant fire. A single page turning, somewhere far off. The soft settle of the old door. The House is alive, not noisy. *(The sky and the world outside make no sound — Layer 0.)*

## IX. Lighting

Two lights, in tension. **Cool moonlight** falls through high windows onto the threshold stone (Law 003 — Moonlight Is Sacred). **One warm fire**, deep inside, glows just out of sight and pulls you forward (Law 004 — Fire Is Life). Between them, comfortable darkness (Law 005). No overhead light. No white light, ever. The visitor should feel drawn *toward the warmth.*

## X. The Arrival *(Discovery / Interaction — Law 007)*

Nothing appears immediately. The screen does not present a menu. For the first several seconds there is only the room. Then the Keeper's half-seen presence and *"Welcome home."* Then — and only then — the gentle fork: *study by the fire* (→ deeper into the Library, toward the Fireplace) or *wander* (→ free to explore). Returning residents are met by name in the House's quiet way; a soul's very first stay eventually opens onto the First Night. **No rush, ever.**

## XI. Hidden Stories

The coat on the hook has hung there longer than anyone remembers; it fits no one and everyone. The page that keeps turning, somewhere out of sight, is never the same page twice. The raven high above has watched every single person who ever crossed this threshold — and has never once been seen to move.

## XII. Things We Refuse To Do

No tutorial. No popup. No menu on arrival. No "Get Started." No celebration animation. No progress bar. No notification. No urgency of any kind. **Nothing that turns arriving into using.**

## XIII. The Final Test

A visitor should be able to cross the threshold, do **nothing at all**, and still feel *welcomed.* If they simply stand there and breathe for ten seconds, the House has already done its job.

## XIV. The House Whispers

> *"Welcome home."*

and, beneath it, the thing the whole room is quietly saying:

> *"Take your coat off. You're safe here."*

## XV. Scene Direction
*A director's brief for the render — the still, waiting Threshold. Paint the room described above.*

Night. We stand just inside a great old doorway, at the height of someone who has just stepped in from the cold. The tall doors are **ajar**, and through the gap and past them, **warm firelight and candleglow spill from deeper inside the House** — we cannot see the fire itself, only its glow laid in a long soft rectangle across worn stone. Ahead, the Library begins: dark walnut shelves climbing up out of the frame into a ceiling lost to shadow, books receding farther than the eye can follow. **High, cold windows** let in the moon (Layer 0 sky beyond — midnight blue, a silver moon, sparse stars), and that cool light crosses the warm light on the floor, **dust drifting slowly** in the beam. Just inside, an iron hook with a single coat left on it. A worn rug; the first stair deeper in. Impossibly high above, almost lost in the dark, **a raven** on a ledge.

The composition **leans inward** — every line, the doorframe, the light on the floor, the shelves — pulls the eye toward the distant warmth, so the first involuntary thought is *"…I want to know what's through those doors."* The feeling is **arrival, safety, permission to slow down.** It is hushed, ancient, unhurried; the House has been waiting and is in no rush.

**Not in the plate:** no people; the Keeper is *not* rendered (she arrives, half-seen, in the experience layer); no cat staged; no text, no UI, no menu, no labels. Cool moonlight + one warm interior source only; comfortable darkness in the corners; ~5% parallax bleed past every edge; leave calm dark air around the doorway, the coat, and the light-path so each can later be touched. Photoreal, gothic-romantic, real materials, volumetric light, natural grain, no lens flare. 16:9.
