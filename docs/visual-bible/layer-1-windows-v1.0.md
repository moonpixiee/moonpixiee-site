# 🪟 Visual Bible — Layer 1 · The Windows
### House of Luna · The Library · Status: **Canon v1.0** · Set Piece 002

> *This isn't a window. This is the House breathing.*

A **creative contract**, not a prompt. Layer 1 sits just in front of the Sky (Layer 0) and behind the Architecture (Layer 2) in the composite — its rain, snow, and condensation are **animated independently**, so the glass lives without the room ever being re-rendered.

---

## Purpose

The windows exist for one reason: to remind the visitor that **the House is part of a much larger world.** Without them, the Library is a *room.* With them, it becomes a *place.*

## Architectural style

Not castle windows — **1,000-year-old cathedral windows that have slowly become part of a library.** Gothic · Victorian · Castle · Dark Academia · Handcrafted. **NOT church. NOT fantasy game. REAL.**

## Scale

**Three** primary windows. The middle is tallest; the two sides slightly smaller. Each stretches almost floor-to-ceiling. They should make the visitor feel **small.**

## Frames

Dark walnut. **Hand-carved**, never machine-perfect — tiny imperfections. Tiny botanical carvings, **hidden ravens, tiny crescents, pressed vines.** Things nobody notices on Day 1.

## Glass

**Hand-blown antique glass.** Nothing perfectly flat: moonlight **bends**, rain **distorts**, stars **shimmer.** The outside world feels slightly **dreamlike.**

## Glass details

**Not** stained-glass windows (too cathedral). Instead, **tiny stained-glass inlays** — very subtle, only around the edges, almost like **jewelry**: 🌙 a crescent moon · 🌿 ivy · 🪶 a raven feather · ⭐ tiny stars.

## Rain *(one of the most important memories)*

Rain doesn't stream. It **lingers.** Tiny droplets slowly collect; sometimes **two drops meet, then race downward together.** Nobody notices consciously. Everyone feels it.

## Snow

Winter only. Huge, fluffy, **slow** flakes. Peaceful. Never a blizzard.

## Storms

Very rare, never scary. Occasionally, far away, **lightning softly illuminates distant trees** — then darkness returns.

## Outside *(important)*

**You never see civilization. Never.** Only ancient trees, castle rooftops, distant mountains, mist, moonlight. Nothing modern. The House exists *somewhere*, but no one knows exactly where.

## The window ledge *(stories, not decoration)*

Everything here has history: one tiny ivy plant · a feather · a brass telescope · a dried flower · one candle · maybe an old key forgotten years ago.

## Colors

Moonlight silver · charcoal · midnight blue · soft violet · muted navy · and **warm amber reflections from the fire** on the inside of the glass.

## Animation

Tiny — almost invisible. Clouds drift, rain falls, snow floats, moonlight slowly changes, branches sway. **Nothing loops obviously. Nothing feels digital.**

## Sound relationship

Rain grows **louder the closer you stand** to the windows — not dramatically, just enough. If someone chooses to stand there, they're **rewarded.** *(LAW 012 — the House reaches toward you; LAW 009 — you hear it before you're told to.)*

## Never

No city lights · no roads · no people · no glowing fantasy forests · no dragons · no castles everywhere · no bright stained glass · no giant moon · no obvious magic. **Wonder comes from restraint.**

---

## Compositing note *(Layer 1 in the stack)*

Sits directly in front of **Layer 0 (Sky)** and behind **Layer 2 (Architecture).** The **frames + glass** are a photoreal render; the **rain / snow / condensation / distant lightning** are the animated overlay that makes the glass *live.* Leave **generous negative space** around the windows so the foreground layers (bookshelves, fireplace, furniture) stack naturally in front.

---

## 🎬 Scene Direction — the *Composed Vision* (the assembled target)

*This describes how Layer 1 reads once the whole stack is assembled — the north-star look (see Luna's concept piece). It is **not** a single render; it's the destination. What we actually render is the isolated asset below.*

> Create three towering Gothic-Victorian library windows crafted from hand-carved dark walnut with subtle botanical carvings, hidden ravens, and crescent moon details. Use antique hand-blown glass with gentle imperfections that softly distort the moonlight beyond. The windows should frame an ancient nighttime landscape of towering trees, distant castle rooftops, and misty mountains under a soft silver moon. Rain droplets cling naturally to the glass and slowly race downward in places, with subtle condensation around the edges. Small stained-glass accents are worked delicately into the borders — not full stained-glass windows, only tiny decorative inlays of ivy, stars, and crescent moons. Moonlight should spill gently through the windows, supporting the room rather than dominating it. The overall feeling should be timeless, quiet, and deeply comforting, as though someone has stood here watching the rain for centuries. Leave generous negative space around the windows so foreground set pieces (bookshelves, fireplace, furniture) can layer naturally in front.
>
> **Design this as though someone will remember it twenty years from now.**

## 📦 Asset Brief — what we actually render *(isolated · transparent · Layer 1 only)*

> Generate **one set of three Gothic-Victorian cathedral window frames with antique hand-blown glass** — the tall centre window and two slightly shorter sides — viewed **straight on, centered, isolated on a transparent background.** Hand-carved dark walnut with subtle botanical carvings, hidden ravens, and crescent inlays worked delicately into the borders. **NO wall. NO sky. NO moon. NO landscape. NO candles. NO books. NO ivy. NO ledge props.** House Standard lighting (cool moon key + faint warm firelight on the inner glass), at rest; generous transparent margins. Design this as though someone will remember it twenty years from now.

**What is NOT part of this asset** *(they are their own layers, so they animate & swap alone):*
- the **moon / stars / clouds / rain landscape** → **Layer 0 (`sky.png`)**, sits *behind* the glass
- the **stone wall the windows are set into** → **Layer 2 (`architecture.png`)**, sits *around* them
- the **candles, telescope, raven, dried flowers, ivy, the forgotten key** on the sill → **Layer 6 props** (each its own sticker)
- the **rain-on-glass, condensation, distant lightning** → the **animated overlay** on this layer (not baked into the frames)

*(Luna's beautiful full-scene concept image is the **Composed Vision** — the target we assemble toward — not the asset we render.)*
